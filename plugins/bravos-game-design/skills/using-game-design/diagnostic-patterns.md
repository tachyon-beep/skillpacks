# Diagnostic Patterns

Use this reference to move from a symptom to competing causes, a discriminating
probe, and the smallest targeted repair. Do not apply a named pattern as a
verdict without checking the live design.

## Contents

- Diagnostic method
- Pattern index
- Distinguish similar symptoms
- Report a diagnosis

## Diagnostic method

1. State the symptom in observable terms.
2. List at least two plausible causes.
3. Choose a probe whose outcomes distinguish those causes.
4. Repair the causal break, not merely the visible symptom.
5. Predict a side effect and test for it.

## Pattern index

| Pattern | Observable symptom | Discriminating probe | Targeted repairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local candy, global porridge | individual actions are pleasant, but later choices and session shape do not change | trace each mechanic to a later affected decision; remove it and compare the loop | persist, expose, price, hand off, cross-couple, settle, or shelve |
| Activity without agency | players perform procedures but cannot alter relevant outcomes or interpretations | compare legal alternatives and downstream state under each | create live trade-offs, consequential performance, or honest automation |
| Many buttons, one decision | option count is high but one rule selects the answer | test options across reachable states, beliefs, and aims | differentiate timing, risk, information, dependency, or remove false options |
| Spectacle without participation | systems generate vivid events while players mainly watch or explain | ablate player actions and see whether the trajectory changes | move control, response, interpretation, or consequence into player decisions |
| Theme as causal substitute | fiction describes tension, trust, or power that rules never require | restate rules without theme and identify actual incentives | add commitment, evidence, cost, capability, or relationship consequence |
| Content treadmill | new cards, levels, prompts, or enemies ask the same solved question | compare action rankings before and after new content | transform constraints, models, relationships, capabilities, or stop adding content |
| Progress without transformation | numbers rise but decisions remain identical | sample early, middle, and late states and compare reasoning | add qualitative thresholds, new dependencies, counters, or compress progression |
| Depth without legibility | a rich model exists, but players cannot perceive causal signals | ask players to predict outcomes and cite state before acting | expose discriminators, improve feedback, reduce hidden coupling, stage learning |
| Complexity debt | rules, components, or exceptions consume attention without distinct jobs | assign each element a job and run removal/substitution tests | merge, automate, externalize, narrow, or delete |
| Dominant route | the same plan remains best across relevant states and opponents | vary information, timing, resources, player aims, and counterplay | create endogenous counters or differentiated trade-offs before numerical nerfs |
| Forced response treadmill | one threat repeatedly demands the same counter | remove or delay the threat and inspect the option space | add multiple responses, partial acceptance, prediction, or threat cost |
| Runaway leader | advantage increases capacity while reducing exposure and counterplay | start from small leads at different phases and project decision quality | add exposure, diminishing returns, contested fronts, or bounded catch-up |
| Catch-up erases authorship | trailing players recover regardless of better prior play | compare outcomes with and without deliberate catch-up decisions | make recovery earned, risky, legible, or reduce initial snowball instead |
| Kingmaker ending | a player unable to pursue their own goal selects the winner | examine whether prior commitments meaningfully constrain the choice | preserve as diplomacy, add self-relevant stakes, distribute decision, or shorten end |
| Quarterbacked cooperation | one player proposes and effectively decides every action | remove that player or privatize one responsibility and observe agency | complementary information, simultaneous ownership, plural goals, bounded advice |
| Multiplayer solitaire | players share time and scores but rarely change one another's choices | draw the cross-player consequence graph | add shared state, prediction, exchange, audience, blocking, or honest parallel play |
| Analysis paralysis | planning time grows faster than meaningful distinction | log options considered, dependencies, and reasons for revision | remove irrelevant branches, stage decisions, shorten horizon, clarify state |
| Dead time | non-acting players cannot predict, prepare, react, or influence | record attention and available consequential activity by seat | parallelize, add reactions or audience, shorten procedures, change topology |
| Randomness erases authorship | careful choices are overwhelmed by late uncontrollable variance | move the random event earlier or expose mitigation and compare | use input variance, bounded outcomes, risk choice, recovery, or remove randomness |
| Randomness without uncertainty play | random results vary content but create no forecast, mitigation, or adaptation | reveal the result earlier and see whether play changes | connect variance to decisions or automate/remove it |
| Hidden information as guessing | players lack evidence that distinguishes concealed possibilities | ask what observable fact could update each belief | add signals, probes, costly claims, patterned distributions, or reveal more |
| Feedback fog | players see an outcome but cannot connect it to action or system | ask for predictions before action and explanations after | isolate signals, stage resolution, expose ownership, reduce delayed coupling |
| Flat pressure | every phase has the same urgency, load, and consequence | chart cognitive, social, physical, and strategic pressure over time | create contrast, accumulation, recovery, thresholds, or a stopping boundary |
| Foregone-conclusion ending | winner or resolution is clear while procedure continues | identify the last point at which a live decision can change the result | trigger closure, concede safely, introduce meaningful final stakes, shorten |
| Onboarding wall | players must understand the whole system before a useful action | list concepts needed for the first three decisions | stage rules, constrain early state, teach through consequence, provide references |
| Facilitator life support | the host invents urgency, rulings, goals, or spotlight every cycle | run from written procedure or with a new facilitator | externalize recurring jobs, clarify authority, provide structures, reduce load |
| Social pressure as mechanic | compliance depends on embarrassment, hierarchy, or inability to refuse | offer a private cost-free refusal and compare behavior | redesign consent and incentives; do not relabel coercion as intensity |
| Ceremonial safety | a tool exists but nobody can or will use it under pressure | rehearse invocation in representative conditions | reduce social cost, improve signal, assign response, add private/accessible routes |
| Applause-driven iteration | feedback is positive but gives no changed design decision | ask for remembered choices, expectations, and failures | precommit claims, observe behavior, seek counterevidence, run blind tests |
| Prototype cathedral | too much game is built before the key uncertainty is tested | name the single riskiest claim and everything not needed to test it | create a slice, mock system boundaries, discard fidelity |
| Metric substitution | an easy measure improves while intended experience remains uncertain | trace the metric to the experience and list alternative causes | measure nearer behavior, triangulate, add guardrails, stop optimizing proxy |
| Automation hides the game | software resolves complex state too quickly for players to learn or care | expose an intermediate trace and compare predictions | surface actionable causality, slow key transitions, let players intervene |
| Novelty-only energy | first sessions are lively but repeated play reveals stable answers | repeat with experienced players and fixed content | deepen interaction and transformation or embrace a one-shot promise |

## Distinguish similar symptoms

### “Players did not use the mechanic”

Test separately whether they:

- noticed the option;
- understood its procedure;
- predicted its consequence;
- valued its consequence;
- encountered a state where it mattered;
- could afford or socially perform it;
- believed the game or group permitted it.

Do not buff the reward until these causes are separated.

### “The game is slow”

Separate:

- physical or interface procedure;
- rules lookup and state parsing;
- deep but engaging deliberation;
- waiting for another player;
- negotiation that changes beliefs;
- repeated resolution after the decision is over;
- weak goals causing drift.

Shorten only the time that does not serve the experience.

### “The game is too random”

Separate variance magnitude, timing, frequency, visibility, mitigation,
distribution, consequence, and mismatch with the promised skill. A player may
accept high variance they chose and reject small variance that invalidates an
earned result.

### “Players are confused”

Separate operation, state, causality, objective, strategy, uncertainty, and
intended ambiguity. Teach operational rules; expose causal signals; preserve
uncertainty that constitutes the play.

### “It was fun but nobody wants another session”

Consider whether the work was satisfying and complete, socially or logistically
costly, novelty-dependent, exhausting, poorly scheduled, or deliberately
one-shot. Do not force replayability when completion is the value. If return is
part of the promise, identify what a second session would transform.

## Report a diagnosis

Use:

```markdown
**Verdict:** [Highest-leverage diagnosis]
**Protected intent:** [What must survive]
**Observed symptom:** [Behavior or rule fact]
**Leading causes:** [Ranked alternatives]
**Discriminating probe:** [Smallest test]
**Repair if confirmed:** [Causal change]
**Side effect to watch:** [New risk]
**Confidence:** [What is known versus inferred]
```
