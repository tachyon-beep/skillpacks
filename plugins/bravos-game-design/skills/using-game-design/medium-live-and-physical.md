# Live and Physical Media

Use this reference for LARP, site-specific play, playground games, physical
games, and sport-like forms where bodies, space, equipment, and co-presence bear
the rules.

## Contents

- LARP and live embodied affordances
- Site, intensity, and organizer labor
- Physical and sport-like affordances
- Ability, equipment, and officiating

## Use LARP and live embodied affordances

Exploit:

- bodies, space, proximity, gesture, costume, voice, material, and duration;
- simultaneous action and dense social networks;
- site-specific meaning, ritual, immersion, and public/private movement;
- performance, identity exploration, and lived relationship.

Pay for:

- physical and emotional risk, fatigue, weather, hygiene, transport, and site
  access;
- casting, costuming, scheduling, staffing, briefing, calibration, and debrief;
- unequal mobility, audibility, visibility, confidence, and social power;
- events that cannot be paused, reproduced, or privately debugged;
- real-world consequences of fictional hierarchy, intimacy, or exclusion.

Check:

- What interaction requires embodiment rather than representation?
- Can every participant perceive and perform their important actions?
- Which spaces create privacy, status, bottlenecks, isolation, or danger?
- How can intensity be increased, reduced, redirected, paused, or exited?
- Who notices and responds when a participant cannot self-advocate?
- How do players distinguish character and participant communication?
- What labor falls on organizers before, during, and after the event?
- What remains between participants when play ends?

Prototype dangerous, intimate, or logistically complex interactions symbolically
before adding fidelity. Walk the site and transitions, not only the scenes.

## Design the site, intensity, and organizer system

Map routes, thresholds, visibility, audibility, privacy, lighting, weather,
sanitation, rest, emergency access, and participant departure. Walk the site
from each role and relevant access need.

Treat casting, briefing, workshops, costuming, props, food, breaks, calibration,
facilitation, welfare, incident response, derole, debrief, cleanup, and follow-up
as parts of the playable system. Assign owners and backups. Do not depend on
participants to discover or negotiate safety boundaries during peak intensity.

Use at least one accessible path to initiate, refuse, redirect, pause, and exit
load-bearing interactions. Do not make volume, stamina, mobility, charisma, or
improvisational confidence hidden universal currencies unless explicitly
intended and supported.

Read [social, narrative, and embodied play](social-narrative-and-embodied-play.md)
and [accessibility, safety, and ethics](accessibility-safety-and-ethics.md) for
intense, intimate, or socially consequential work.

## Use physical and sport-like affordances

Exploit:

- movement, timing, coordination, aim, endurance, balance, contact, and space;
- instantly legible performance and spectatorship;
- team coordination and embodied mastery;
- simple rules with deep execution.

Pay for:

- injury, fatigue, equipment, venue, weather, supervision, and emergency access;
- large differences in ability, body, age, training, and risk tolerance;
- ambiguous physical outcomes and officiating;
- exclusion created by speed, contact, strength, or endurance requirements.

Check:

- Which physical skill is the game intentionally testing?
- Can intensity and contact be calibrated without destroying the activity?
- Are boundaries, fouls, starts, stops, ties, and emergencies legible?
- Does equipment fail safely and fit different bodies?
- Can roles or rules preserve agency across ability differences?
- Does elimination create unsafe overexertion or prolonged exclusion?
- What does a referee observe, and can players challenge a ruling?

Do not disguise avoidable danger as authenticity or competitive purity.

## Design ability, equipment, and officiating

Separate intended physical challenge from incidental barriers. If speed is the
skill, state it; if coordination, flocking, tactics, or rhythm is the skill,
avoid letting speed decide it accidentally. Use roles, zones, pacing, equipment,
or scoring that reward the intended performance.

Specify equipment size, weight, material, failure mode, inspection, fit, hygiene,
storage, and replacement. Define boundaries and emergency stops visibly. Train
officials on both game state and participant welfare.

For mixed ability, age, or experience, test whether adaptations preserve
meaningful contribution and dignity. Do not force one participant to become the
permanent helper, target, or exception.
