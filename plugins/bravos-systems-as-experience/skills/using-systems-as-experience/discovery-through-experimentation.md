# Discoverable mechanics and feedback

Use when players should learn mechanics through observations and experiments. Obscurity is not a substitute for depth.

## Checks

- Specify what a player can initially observe, what hypothesis they could form and the experiment that distinguishes it.
- Make cause/effect and unexpected outcomes legible; prevent unrelated noise from making the intended inference impossible.
- Compare implicit hints, demonstrations, journals and direct instruction for the actual audience/task. A tutorial is not inherently a failure.
- Preserve safe/recoverable experiments where the intended experience needs them; severe irreversible penalties can suppress exploration.
- Check whether knowledge changes decisions or merely unlocks a memorized answer. Avoid invalidating prior evidence without a discoverable reason.
- Test relevant novices/experienced players and distinguish designer knowledge from observed discovery. Synthetic walkthroughs cannot establish an aha moment.

## Deliverable

Knowledge/hypothesis path, feedback prototype, alternative interpretation and human observation/playtest plan. See [sandbox](sandbox-design-patterns.md), [emergence](emergent-gameplay-design.md) and [narrative](player-driven-narratives.md).
