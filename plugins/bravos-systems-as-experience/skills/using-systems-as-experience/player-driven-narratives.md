# State consequences and interpretable stories

Use when persistent simulation consequences should support player interpretation and memorable events.

## Checks

- Define persistent character/world state, relationships, commitments and event consequences; random event volume is not narrative coherence.
- Trace causal history/provenance so outcomes can be understood. Surface relevant causes without prescribing every emotional interpretation.
- Check pacing and recovery, recurring character/goal continuity and whether generated events conflict with established facts or authored constraints.
- Keep player agency and the limits of control explicit; distinguish consequences of decisions from arbitrary punishment.
- Preserve save/replay identity and event ordering where history matters. Logs and summaries need source anchors rather than fabricated continuity.
- Prototype a small event chain and ask relevant humans what they recall/understand. Synthetic story retellings are design hypotheses, not evidence of memorable player experience.

## Deliverable

State/event/cause contract, bounded scenario prototype, continuity checks and human observation/playtest evidence or plan. See [emergence](emergent-gameplay-design.md), [discovery](discovery-through-experimentation.md) and [community](community-meta-gaming.md).
