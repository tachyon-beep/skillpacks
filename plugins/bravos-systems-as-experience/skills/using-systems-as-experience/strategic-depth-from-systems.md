# Strategic choice and counterplay

Use when combinations, asymmetry or progression should support meaningful strategic decisions. Combinatorial size is not depth.

## Checks

- State the recurring decision, available information and consequences. Separate execution skill, planning and hidden knowledge demands.
- Map feasible strategies and counterplay under task/map/opponent conditions; inspect dominant choices and false alternatives.
- Check synergy/cost/opportunity-cost relationships and mutually exclusive commitments. More stats or branches can increase complexity without changing choices.
- Evaluate asymmetry through actual matchups and readable strengths/weaknesses, not faction theme alone.
- Test adaptation, discovery and recovery; a solved local scenario may coexist with a strategically rich larger game.
- Observe relevant players over appropriate experience levels. Aggregate win rates or synthetic optimal play alone cannot establish perceived agency or depth.

## Deliverable

Decision/counterplay map, evidence for a material gap and a bounded prototype/playtest comparison. See [emergence](emergent-gameplay-design.md), [optimization](optimization-as-play.md) and [community metagame](community-meta-gaming.md).
