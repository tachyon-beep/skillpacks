# Documentation Clarity Review

Review against the reader's task and actual source, not a generic prose score.
Keep terms, claims, units and conditions precise; preserve the author's useful voice.

- Lead with what the reader needs to decide or do.
- Prefer concrete actors/actions and examples grounded in the real system.
- Define unfamiliar terms at first consequential use; preserve official names.
- Put prerequisites before actions and expected results after them.
- Separate facts, rationale, proposals and approval status.
- Remove repeated scaffolding and content that does not serve the task.
- Use headings/lists/tables where they improve retrieval, not to fill a template.

Active voice is usually clear, but institutional register and emphasis may justify
another form. Do not force one style on policy, executive, academic and operational
artifacts; use [editorial-registers.md](editorial-registers.md) when those differences
matter. A rewrite request authorizes rewriting within scope; a critique request
alone does not authorize replacing the document.

For a review, quote the decisive passage, explain the reader consequence and offer
a focused correction. Validate factual and executable claims separately with
[documentation-testing.md](documentation-testing.md).
