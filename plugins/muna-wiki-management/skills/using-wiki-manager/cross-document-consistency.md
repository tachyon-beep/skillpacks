# Cross-Document Consistency

Check consequential claims and terminology against current canonical sources.
Registries aid retrieval but are not objective truth by themselves; verify their
currency and declared authority. A scoped audit can use sources directly without
first constructing a full registry system.

## Useful records

A term record can store canonical definition, source, allowed audience variants
and prohibited conflations. A claim record can store stable ID, exact meaning,
source/version/location, qualifiers and affected derivative locations. Track only
claims whose inconsistency matters; no minimum entry count applies.

## Checks

- Preserve counts, units, timeframe, population, conditions and normative force.
- Distinguish current/proposed/deprecated/approved states across documents.
- Terms may legitimately vary by audience; compare meaning, not only byte equality.
- Verify actual link targets/anchors and inbound references after structural edits.
- Inspect source conflicts and unsupported new inference separately from typos.
- Check audience paths where missing definitions or content block the task.

Use existing search, structured parsing and link validators. Numeric search can
find candidates but cannot determine whether two occurrences express the same
claim. Example placeholders are not broken product links.

Return location, conflicting source/derivative meaning, reader consequence and
correction. Prioritize wrong consequential information before degraded navigation
or minor friction. Report scanned scope, samples and unresolved authority rather
than claim universal consistency from one pass. See
[document-evolution.md](document-evolution.md) for propagation.
