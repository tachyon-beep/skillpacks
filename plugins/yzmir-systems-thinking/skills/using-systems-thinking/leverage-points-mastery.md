# Intervention lenses and feasibility

Use Meadows' leverage-point hierarchy to generate alternative mechanisms, not to rank proposals without evidence.

## Lenses

Consider parameter/buffer changes, physical stock-flow structure, delays, balancing/reinforcing feedback, information flows, rules, self-organization, goals and paradigms. These lenses describe how an intervention might work; abstractness does not establish effect size, feasibility or safety.

## Comparison checks

- Define the decision, observed mechanism and constraints. A direct local fix may be sufficient even when a larger system exists.
- Compare no-change, a small reversible intervention and plausible structural alternatives.
- Record evidence, delay, dependencies, implementation risk, authority, reversibility and possible counteracting loops.
- Estimate effects only from supported parameters or ranges; a hierarchy position cannot supply a missing causal estimate.
- Identify the signal that would show the intervention failed or activated an unintended mechanism. Plan a bounded rollout/experiment where appropriate.
- Lower-level changes can be the right action under time, budget or evidence constraints. Do not treat this as a rationalization to overcome.

## Deliverable

Feasible intervention comparison with assumptions, counterevidence, expected observations and next decision. See [causal maps](causal-loop-diagramming.md) and [stock-flow models](stocks-and-flows-modeling.md).
